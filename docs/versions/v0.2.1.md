# v0.2.1 — what the emotion vocabulary is for

**Status:** noted, not scheduled · **Written:** 2026-08-30 · **Follows:** v0.2.0

A small version between v0.2.0 and v0.3.0, written now because the observation is fresh and the
dependency between its two halves is the kind that is expensive to discover late.

## The observation

**The emotions are not working.** Driving v0.2 against a real endpoint, she rarely reaches for a
stance at all, and the ones she does reach for look close to arbitrary — a `curious` where the
answer is plainly confident, a `warm` on a correction. [ADR 3](../adr/0003-emotions-as-tool-calls.md)
is right that a stance is a tool call rather than something parsed back out of prose, and that part
is not in question. What is in question is everything above it: whether the vocabulary means
anything to the model, and whether the feature is shaped so that using it well is easier than
using it badly.

Three explanations, and they are not exclusive:

- **Fourteen stances, and several of them are the same occasion.** The glosses in
  `DEFAULT_EMOTIONS` are already written as occasions rather than as feelings, which is the right
  shape — but `disagree`, `doubt` and `judge` all fire on *I think this is wrong*, and `curious`,
  `excited` and `hope` all fire on *this is going somewhere*. When several entries fit, which one
  gets picked is noise, and from outside it looks like the model choosing at random. It is.
- **One of them is now a tool.** The vocabulary still contains `ask` — *"You need something from
  them to go further"* — which is exactly what `hera__ask` does, and has done since v0.2 M1. A
  stance and a tool competing for the same occasion is the overlap ADR 12 removed between `note`
  and the scratchpad, in a place nobody went back to look.
- **The feature may be shaped wrongly.** A stance is described as something to call *alongside*
  prose, as often as is honest — a request to interrupt an answer several times. A model that
  writes fluently is being asked to stop and file a small form, and it either forgets or complies
  mechanically.

## What could be done, in the order the options get bigger

1. **Merge the overlaps and drop `ask`.** The cheapest change and the one with an argument behind
   it rather than a hunch: no two stances should fire on the same occasion, and none of them should
   compete with a tool. This is a `DEFAULT_EMOTIONS` edit and nothing else.
2. **A shorter vocabulary.** Four or five stances that are genuinely distinguishable, with the rest
   available as invented kinds — which ADR 3 already permits and the card already renders.
3. **A stance as an argument rather than a call.** One optional field on the answer instead of a
   separate round trip. Costs the parallel-call trick ADR 3 was built around, and needs a real
   answer to *where does it live if the model does not emit one*.
4. **Drop the tool.** Honest if none of the above lands. What is lost is a real thing — the openness
   the card gives a person about how she is holding what she says — so it is last rather than first.

## The dependency that orders the work

**`hera__ask` has to stand on its own before any of this.** It is already a separate tool with its
own description, and that is not what needs moving: what couples it to the emotions is its `kind`
argument, which reads from the same `emotions.json` vocabulary, is documented in terms of it, and
picks the colour `QuestionCard` is drawn in. Shortening or removing the vocabulary breaks a
question card that has nothing to do with stances.

So: **decouple `ask` first**, then change the emotions. What that means concretely —

- `hera__ask(question, kind)` keeps a `kind`, but from its own small closed set about the *question*
  rather than about her mood: something like `unsure · blocked · choice`. Those are the three
  occasions the `uncertainty` mind region actually describes.
- `AnswerRequired.kind` stops being documented as "the same open vocabulary `hera__emotion` draws
  on", and `QuestionCard.svelte:44` stops resolving its tone through `workspace.emotions` — today
  a question whose `kind` is not in the vocabulary is drawn with no tone at all, which is the
  breakage this ordering avoids.
- Only then is `emotions.json` free to shrink, change or go.

Nothing here is decided. When it is, it wants an ADR that supersedes 3 rather than an edit to it —
3 is accepted and was right about the mechanism, and a record quietly rewritten teaches nothing.

## Not in this version

Everything else. If it grows past the two changes above it is not a point release; the rest belongs
in v0.3.
