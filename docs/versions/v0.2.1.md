# v0.2.1 — the polish pass

**Status:** planned · **Written:** 2026-08-30, rewritten 2026-08-31 · **Follows:** v0.2.0

v0.2.0 made Hera **accumulate**: projects to file work under, a scratchpad she thinks on, artifacts
she publishes, and memory that survives a conversation. Four capabilities, and every one of them
arrived with its interface written to be *correct* rather than to be argued with.

This is the release that argues with it. Everything here is quality of life: the same features,
easier to live with — plus one small tool the last release owes the model, and a vocabulary that has
not been working.

**Why a patch release and not v0.3.** Nothing here changes the shape of the system. There is no new
package, no new table, no new decision about what Hera *is* — v0.3 has those
([v0.3.0.md](v0.3.0.md): dreaming, a sandbox, `hera-code`). Splitting them means the interface work
can ship in a week without waiting behind a container runtime.

---

## 1. `hera__read_resource` — the sentence that is currently untrue

**First, because it is the only thing here that is a lie rather than a rough edge.** Every selected
skill with files beside it arrives in the prompt with *"This skill has further files beside it in
`<path>`. Read one if this skill tells you to."* She has no tool that can read a file. This is the
actual reason Anthropic's reference-heavy skills do not work, and it is one small tool away from
being true.

[ADR 14](../adr/0014-skill-resources-are-readable.md) is written and accepted:
`hera__read_resource(skill, path)` behind a `ResourceReader` port, refusing `..`, absolute paths and
anything that escapes the skill's directory once symlinks are resolved. **The guard already exists
twice and must not exist a third time** — `hera_core.chat_files._resolve` is the same check for a
chat's directories, and its parametrised test class is what this extends.

It was planned as M2b's second half and lifted out cleanly, because the two shared nothing but that
guard. `brand-guidelines`, `canvas-design`, `dataviz`, `frontend-design` and
`web-artifacts-builder` are the skills this unblocks: they read `references/*.md` and emit a
document, which is exactly artifacts plus this. The script-running ones (`pptx`, `docx`, `xlsx`)
still need [ADR 15](../adr/0015-running-code-in-a-container.md) and stay v0.3.

## 2. The redesign pass, moved from v0.2.0

[`frontend.md`](../frontend.md) says the design language gets adjusted once there is something to
argue with. There is now, and there is *more* of it than when that sentence was written — which is
why this reads better here than as M3 of the last release.

**The argument goes into that document first and is built second.** It is the one place this project
decides interface questions, and a change made in a component and back-filled into the doc is a
change nobody could disagree with before it existed.

- **The rail carries more than it was drawn for** — projects with menus and colour, chats,
  a search entry, and eventually dreams. It wants the real hierarchy and the collapse-to-icons
  behaviour `frontend.md` § Chat already draws and the build does not have.
- **The transcript and the activity gutter** are the two densest components in the interface and
  where every new event variant lands. What wants settling: the rhythm between an answer, a card and
  a gutter block, and where an artifact card sits relative to the prose that produced it. Six glyphs
  now share that gutter — scroll, quill, brain, stele, globe, wrench — and they were drawn one at a
  time, one per milestone. They have never been looked at together.
- **The mobile sheet.** The interface is installable as a PWA and is desktop-shaped.

**The drawer is not in this list**, and that is worth saying: it was M3's load-bearing claim — *one
drawer, three panels* — and it was built early with artifacts because that is the panel that needed
it. Memory reuses the frame rather than bringing its own, which is the proof the claim wanted.

## 3. Hotkeys, and `⌘K` in particular

**`⌘K` opens Settings today and should open a command palette.** It is listed as a known gap in both
the changelog and the status document, and the rail mock has said `🔍 Search ⌘K` since before there
was a rail. What it should reach: a chat by name, a project, a skill, a settings screen, and *new
chat*.

Beyond it there is no keyboard story at all. The composer has `Enter` and `Shift+Enter`, and
everything else is a mouse. What is worth having is small and worth deciding in `frontend.md` rather
than one component at a time: moving between conversations, opening and closing the drawer,
switching a memory off from the settings list, and closing whatever is open — the last one because
`Escape` currently means different things in three places.

**One rule this cannot break.** Every shortcut is a *control that also exists on screen*. A feature
reachable only by a chord is a feature nobody discovers, and it is the interface equivalent of the
thing ADR 5 refuses for skills — a mechanism that only works when somebody already knows it is
there.

## 4. What the emotion vocabulary is for — ✅ decided, as [ADR 17](../adr/0017-a-stance-is-a-sentence-and-a-question-stands-alone.md)

**Option 4. The tool is gone, and `ask` was detached first, in that order.** This section is kept
in full below, because it is the observation the decision rests on and a record quietly rewritten
teaches nothing.

What was decided against the four options: **1 and 2 do not fix it.** The occasions overlap because
feelings overlap, so a four-word list has the same problem with fewer words — and neither option
touches the third explanation, which is not about the vocabulary at all. **3 is the thing to reach
for if the loss turns out to matter**, and it still needs a real answer to *where does it live when
the model does not emit one*. So 4, with the cost named rather than argued away: what goes is the
openness the card gave a person about how she was holding what she said.

The dependency below held exactly as written. `ask` keeps a `kind`, from its own closed set —
`unsure · blocked · choice` — and it is a `Literal` in the tool's input schema rather than prose in
a prompt section, which is the part this section did not anticipate: closing the set in the *schema*
means the model cannot get it wrong, where a documented free string only means it was told.

---

**The observation, as written while building v0.2.**

**The emotions are not working.** Driving v0.2 against a real endpoint, she rarely reaches for a
stance at all, and the ones she does reach for look close to arbitrary — a `curious` where the
answer is plainly confident, a `warm` on a correction. [ADR 3](../adr/0003-emotions-as-tool-calls.md)
is right that a stance is a tool call rather than something parsed back out of prose, and that part
is not in question. What is in question is everything above it: whether the vocabulary means
anything to the model, and whether the feature is shaped so that using it well is easier than using
it badly.

Three explanations, and they are not exclusive:

- **Fourteen stances, and several of them are the same occasion.** The glosses in
  `DEFAULT_EMOTIONS` are already written as occasions rather than as feelings, which is the right
  shape — but `disagree`, `doubt` and `judge` all fire on *I think this is wrong*, and `curious`,
  `excited` and `hope` all fire on *this is going somewhere*. When several entries fit, which one
  gets picked is noise, and from outside it looks like the model choosing at random. It is.
- **One of them is now a tool.** The vocabulary still contains `ask` — *"You need something from
  them to go further"* — which is exactly what `hera__ask` does, and has done since v0.2 M1. A
  stance and a tool competing for the same occasion is the overlap ADR 12 removed between `note`
  and the scratchpad, in a place nobody went back to look.
- **The feature may be shaped wrongly.** A stance is described as something to call *alongside*
  prose, as often as is honest — a request to interrupt an answer several times. A model that
  writes fluently is being asked to stop and file a small form, and it either forgets or complies
  mechanically.

### What could be done, in the order the options get bigger

1. **Merge the overlaps and drop `ask`.** The cheapest change and the one with an argument behind
   it rather than a hunch: no two stances should fire on the same occasion, and none of them should
   compete with a tool. This is a `DEFAULT_EMOTIONS` edit and nothing else.
2. **A shorter vocabulary.** Four or five stances that are genuinely distinguishable, with the rest
   available as invented kinds — which ADR 3 already permits and the card already renders.
3. **A stance as an argument rather than a call.** One optional field on the answer instead of a
   separate round trip. Costs the parallel-call trick ADR 3 was built around, and needs a real
   answer to *where does it live if the model does not emit one*.
4. **Drop the tool.** Honest if none of the above lands. What is lost is a real thing — the openness
   the card gives a person about how she is holding what she says — so it is last rather than first.

### The dependency that orders the work

**`hera__ask` has to stand on its own before any of this.** It is already a separate tool with its
own description, and that is not what needs moving: what couples it to the emotions is its `kind`
argument, which reads from the same `emotions.json` vocabulary, is documented in terms of it, and
picks the colour `QuestionCard` is drawn in. Shortening or removing the vocabulary breaks a question
card that has nothing to do with stances.

So: **decouple `ask` first**, then change the emotions. What that means concretely —

- `hera__ask(question, kind)` keeps a `kind`, but from its own small closed set about the *question*
  rather than about her mood: something like `unsure · blocked · choice`. Those are the three
  occasions the `uncertainty` mind region actually describes.
- `AnswerRequired.kind` stops being documented as "the same open vocabulary `hera__emotion` draws
  on", and `QuestionCard.svelte:44` stops resolving its tone through `workspace.emotions` — today
  a question whose `kind` is not in the vocabulary is drawn with no tone at all, which is the
  breakage this ordering avoids.
- Only then is `emotions.json` free to shrink, change or go.

*(Nothing here was decided when it was written. It is now:
[ADR 17](../adr/0017-a-stance-is-a-sentence-and-a-question-stands-alone.md), which supersedes 3
rather than editing it — 3 was right about the mechanism, and that part is untouched and still
binding.)*

## Not in v0.2.1

- **Dreaming, the sandbox, agents and `hera-code`** — all [v0.3.0](v0.3.0.md). Anything that adds a
  package or a decision about what Hera *is* belongs there, which is the line that makes this a
  patch release.
- **`hera__fetch` and PDFs**, both with their reasoning in [`tooling.md`](../tooling.md) § 1 and § 6.
- **Mermaid.** Still a 2–3 MB browser dependency and a second sanitising path; an `.svg` artifact is
  a drawn chart today.

## Verification

The check loop from [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), and per part:

- **`read_resource`** — drop a reference-heavy Anthropic skill into `~/.hera/skills/` and confirm it
  reaches the skill's `references/` and refuses `../`, an absolute path, and a symlink pointing out.
- **The redesign** — the existing Playwright specs still pass, and the new ones are written against
  what `frontend.md` says rather than against what the components do. Watch for the blank page with
  no error, which is the reactive loop; there are three shapes of it recorded now.
- **Hotkeys** — every shortcut has a visible control that does the same thing, checked by opening
  the screen and looking rather than by a test.
- **Emotions** — nothing to verify against a live model any more: the tool is gone, and what is
  left is an absence with a test on it. What *does* want a real endpoint is the other half —
  whether she reaches for `hera__ask` on the right occasions now that its three kinds are about the
  question rather than about her mood, and whether the prose she writes carries the stance the card
  used to. A dozen turns, and the thing to watch for is her hedging in a *new* way now that
  `emotion_usage` is not telling her when a reaction is worth showing.
